A centralized computer runs humanity after a decimating global war.

With this premise, Dick interrogates the effectiveness of withholding information from analysis. Direct information, such as assassinations or riots, should be correlated with economic and social aggregations of data. If the correlations are known beforehand, then the withholding of data leads to a questioning of the reliability of the datasource rather than the proper masking of the sensitive data. 
<!--more-->
In modern times, we saw this exact situation occur, with the COVID pandemic, indicators such as traffic flow, air pollution, emissions from crematories, etc. gave indicators of how severe the COVID infections and deaths were in different regions, even if more direct measures, such as hospital admissions or mortality data was withheld. This I think is an important question to ask, particularly when we ask what is being excluded from our AI models? 

This website for example states noindex and nofollow, but does that mean that the robots will listen. Folk knowledge is information which is not digital, so that information, is precluded from getting incorporated into aggregated datasets, which could maintain the knowledge, but also leads to it not being considered. This is major point of contention for the UN's Biodiversity reporting. 

Dick also comments on what happens when there are multiple machines, which are aiming towards similar goals with slightly different parameterization or paths. One could favor a step decent to the solution, while the other prefers the meandering path. Doing both at once would potentially lead to a entirely different trajectory. Contradictions between potential 'solutions' are rampant and selecting bits and pieces of both, as done in Vulcan's Hammer, could lead to divergence of the system (not that centralized planning is probably a great idea in the first place).
