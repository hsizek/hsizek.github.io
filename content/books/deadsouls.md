The title says it all... Well along with the context that Gogol is critiquing the serfdom of the Russian Empire and the legal and financial structures that lead to the emancipation of the surfs. If you want to know more about this, just read the book, it's a political satire, so it is fun and lighthearted.  
<!--more-->
I didn't really like the censor's ending, but who does. It would have been appropriate to end the book a bit short when Chichinov fled town, forgotten with the new governor general. 

The depictions of table legs was excellent. Short and stout just like the table's master. Other passages clued you into Gogol's proclivities, without outright stating them. This provides an important reminder that the sanitation of history only lives at the surface. Importantly, the censors of the day had more qualms with the presentation of the state, rather than the lives of the individuals.
