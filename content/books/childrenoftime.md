This Czajkowski (Tcaikovsky) is an easy, but long read. Insightful towards how societies evolve and pass knowledge through time. The power in this book is to pose alternatives, just as most science fiction does. There is the standard commentary about how ecological depletion and damage is the scourge that mars this earth, and that systems optimized for one thing, whether it be humans or ants, are not going to result in a happy conclusion. The less standard argument is found in the title, **the Children of Time**. What does it mean to move through time, and how does moving through time alter the course of events. 
<!--more -->

The spaceships, designed to carry frozen Cargo, the humans, to settle new worlds, literally pauses the passage of time for individuals, particularly those who carry knowledge. The benefit is that the stasis does not require mass amounts of energy. Temporarily frozen beings don't need food and a functioning atmosphere. But the cost is that the knowledge doesn't advance, the Cargo acts as a cargo for knowledge and the way society should work, rather than an active source of being for the limited few who are just maintaining the ship. 
The population of a functional plant, passes on information via careful documentation and lineages, which yes can be destroyed, but there is not this temporal skipping. The chain of authority is fairly linear, at least for awhile. The present state uses the past states, but it is not dominated by it. The past merely integrates and informs the present, whispers and recollections.  

The ants are mostly commenting on labor. Though there's many items that could be gleamed, the centralization of labor was I think the most interesting. The ants provided a mechanism for specialization, but specialization also lead to generalization to an extent, with the ability to reprogram the chemical instructions. Domination was not the goal, as domination would require the loss of the ability to give generalized instructions. Hand holding is not needed, but the class of instructions needs to be narrow enough to be effective. This perhaps points out the flaws in silica computers, the hand must always be held and as we are starting to observe our ability to say do this task, needs more specialized physical constraints. The physicality of the ants provide enough structure to decentralize the labor, while the instruction profiles can be enough to constrain the possible outcomes.
