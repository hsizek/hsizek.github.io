The media has dubbed this to be the AI encyclical, but [Magnifica Humanitas](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html) is more so a porting of [Rerum Novarum](https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiii/en/encyclicals/documents/hf_l-xiii_enc_15051891_rerum-novarum.html) into the era of automation via digital computers and a lesser extent LLMs (generative AI). 
<!--more -->

Referencing Benedict XVI, Leo XIV states the capacity of the state is degraded by the capitalization of the market, and thus the ability of the state to address social problems is degraded by increase in privatization of markets. The one aspect which Leo XIV seemed positive about state function was the role in maintaining educational institutions, with an explicit mention of the role of government in education. 
Other classical functions of state, particularly regulation of markets, were stated as functions of a vague, 'community'. This implied that the current operations of states are not promoting social objectives of the Church writ large, with the social objective primarily focused on the maintenance of communities.  Leo XIV does not have the highest faith in the current institutions which compromise modern states to fulfill the demands of the current moment, particularly with the privatization of social functions by the market and the increasing capitalization of value. 

To this end, Leo XIV is clear on how the military industrial complex is used to concentrate wealth via state structures. The role of AI in warfare further expedites this concentration of wealth, while muddling decision making processes and ethical boundaries. Because of the increasing role of capital in war, the effectiveness of the state to moderate the ethics of war is reduced. Increases in automated systems, principally AI, exaggerate existing systems rather than minimizing conflict. The military industrial complex is maintained via state structures which oppose the resolution of conflict. Further, the state's monopoly on the use of force is weakened through this process (capacity of state is degraded via capitalization), leading to persistent conflict. 

From a technology studies perspective, Leo XIV aligns with sociotechnical systems theory rather than technological determinism. This is not all too surprising, but places the burden on the developers and researchers who are building tools and methods to help guide the implementation of those tools. The developer must imagine the applications across sectors and provide a guiding hand, rather than deterministically advancing products. The imagined future must be broad and imaginative. 

The data as a mechanism for colonialism is an interesting theoretical idea, which I'm not well versed in. Ideas are certainly present in literature and media, but implications are not always clear. Data acts as the mediating agent to transfer wealth across the world, enabling actors with data to extract value based on the content of the data, or to generate new data to be sold, whether or not that new data has additional information. The extreme state of this ported by a friend is that every action can have some polymarket bid placed on it, the value of thought becomes a futures projection or a bid. More seriously, the presence of data as a colonial entity enshrines forced labor conditions based on the processing and manipulation of collected data. 

I'll end with the comment 'that promises of automatic general prosperity often prove to be illusions.' This is at the core of the entire encyclical, there is no easy way to repair the wall of Nehemiah, it must be done with all of the communities which make up the city working in a mixed state of isolation and collective. Nurturing communities is more important that developing tools or developing wealth. Many stones can form an arch, singly none.
